Fine-tuned transformer encoders now dominate natural language inference (NLI), but scoring well on held-out test data does not by itself show that a model has learned the compositional rule behind an inference rather than picked up on a superficial statistical shortcut. This paper looks at systematicity, the capacity to generalize compositionally to novel combinations of familiar parts, across five fine-tuned encoder architectures (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa-v3, ALBERT, ModernBERT) and, as a further point of comparison, a general-purpose instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) used zero-shot. Following Goodwin, Sinha and O’Donnell (2020), all models are trained and evaluated on a small artificial language in which the seven natural-logic relations of MacCartney and Manning (2009) are fully determined by an explicit interpretation function, which makes it possible to build controlled probes for generalization to novel vocabulary, sensitivity to a single closed-class perturbation, and consistency under premise/hypothesis reversal.
